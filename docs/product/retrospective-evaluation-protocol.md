# Draft retrospective evaluation protocol

This is a planning template. It does not classify the work as research, audit, or service
evaluation and does not authorise access to clinical data.

## Working question

Can routinely recorded theatre, laboratory, analyser, and communication events reconstruct the
intraoperative OSNA pathway consistently enough to support service audit and improvement?

## Objectives

1. Measure whether the required pathway events and linkage identifiers are available.
2. Quantify missing, duplicate, contradictory, and incorrectly ordered records.
3. Describe transport, laboratory, assay, communication, acknowledgement, and total recorded
   pathway durations without inventing clinical targets.
4. Describe initial runs, repeats, QC failures, and the run linked to laboratory verification.
5. Assess whether the reconstructed evidence is useful to clinical, laboratory, and audit owners.

## Measures

Primary feasibility measures should include:

- percentage of records with usable case, procedure, specimen, and assay-run identifiers;
- percentage of specimens with each required event;
- percentage of complete, incomplete, and invalid reconstructed pathways;
- repeat-run and failed-QC frequency; and
- result-communication and acknowledgement record availability.

Descriptive timing measures should report denominators, missingness, exclusions, median, and
distribution. Threshold or breach analysis must be added only after a locally approved definition
exists.

## Analysis safeguards

- Pre-specify cohort and exclusion rules before inspecting outcome distributions.
- Keep source-validation rejection separate from linkage and pathway-quality findings.
- Do not treat a missing extract record as proof that the clinical event did not occur.
- Exclude invalid pathways from aggregate timing statistics.
- Report incomplete-pathway contribution and missing values explicitly.
- Preserve every source identifier and transformation version in the governed environment.
- Review unexpected codes and temporal patterns with source-system owners.

## Publication planning

A publishable report would need:

- a clearly classified and approved project;
- a reproducible cohort definition and data-flow description;
- source-system and workflow context;
- transparent missing-data and linkage reporting;
- appropriately limited claims about operational records rather than patient outcomes;
- statistical review, including small-sample and multi-site considerations; and
- approval for disclosure of aggregate results and organisational details.

The first useful publication may be a feasibility or service-evaluation methods paper. Clinical
outcome or comparative-effectiveness claims are outside the present dataset and product boundary.
